08 — For students

Built for fellow law students too

A quiet corner for classmates and juniors — resources, a short FAQ, and an easy way to share this page.

Notes from the path

  • Sit in court early. Watching daily civil and criminal proceedings teaches procedure faster than textbooks alone.
  • Treat legal aid as training. Outreach and research for a university legal aid cell build drafting, clarity, and public communication.
  • Write everything down. Hearing notes, filing structures, and citation habits matter as much as the internship certificate.
  • Keep a clean portfolio. One page with CV, experience, and contact is enough — update it when you finish each internship.

Quick questions

How do I start building a law portfolio as a student?

Keep one page with your education, internships, leadership roles, and a CV download. Add certificates as PDFs. Update dates after every internship. Avoid client names and case details.

What should I learn from a District Court internship?

Focus on how filings move, how hearings are called, and how advocates manage procedure. Take notes on formats and stages of trial. Describe the work in general terms when you publish it.

Is legal aid cell work useful for litigation?

Yes. Community awareness, research support, and outreach train you to explain law clearly and work with people — skills that transfer to chambers and firms.

Can fellow students connect?

Yes. This site is for practitioners and fellow law students. Use LinkedIn or the contact section when the email address is published.
